# The 1st and 2nd aorist

## The 1st aorist
The 1st aorist adds the tense suffix -σα to the verb stem (Smyth §542). Most verbs form their aorist this way.

{{table verb:λύω aor.act}}

{{table verb:λύω aor.mid}}

The σ of the suffix combines with the end of the stem (Smyth §544–545):
- A labial and σ make ψ, a velar and σ make ξ: {{form verb:πέμπω aor.act.ind.1.sg}}, {{form verb:πράττω aor.act.ind.1.sg}}.
- A dental is lost before σ: {{form verb:πείθω aor.act.ind.1.sg}}.
- Stems in λ, μ, ν or ρ lose the σ and lengthen their vowel instead: {{form verb:μένω aor.act.ind.1.sg}}, {{form verb:φαίνω aor.act.ind.1.sg}}.

## The 2nd aorist
The 2nd aorist has no tense suffix. It adds the thematic vowel straight to the verb stem, often a shorter form of the stem than the present has (Smyth §546–547). Its indicative has the endings of the imperfect, so the two differ only in the stem:

{{table verb:λείπω aor.act}}

- Imperfect {{form verb:λείπω impf.act.ind.1.sg}}, aorist {{form verb:λείπω aor.act.ind.1.sg}}.
- The middle works the same way: {{form verb:λείπω impf.mp.ind.1.sg aor.mid.ind.1.sg}}.
- The 2nd aorist infinitive is accented on its ending: {{form verb:λείπω aor.act.inf}}. See [infinitives](infinitives).

A verb usually has only one kind of aorist, but a few have both: {{form verb:φέρω aor.act.ind.1.sg}}. The aorist is a past tense and takes the [augment](augment) in the indicative.
