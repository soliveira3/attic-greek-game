# The perfect

The perfect describes an action that is complete, with its result still in place. Its stem is marked by reduplication (Smyth §439), and it is a primary tense, so it takes no augment (Smyth §360).

## Reduplication
- A verb beginning with a single consonant repeats that consonant with ε (Smyth §440): {{form verb:λύω perf.act.ind.1.sg}}. An aspirated consonant is repeated as the matching plain one (Smyth §441): {{form verb:θύω perf.act.ind.1.sg}}.
- A verb beginning with a vowel lengthens it, as in the augment (Smyth §442): {{form verb:ἄγω perf.act.ind.1.sg}}.

## Perfect middle and passive
The endings are added straight to the reduplicated stem, without a thematic vowel (Smyth §574). One set of forms serves the middle and the passive.

{{table verb:λύω perf.mp}}

When the stem ends in a consonant, the consonant changes before the endings: {{form verb:λείπω perf.mp.ind.1.sg}}, {{form verb:πέμπω perf.mp.ind.1.sg}}. The 3rd plural of such verbs was made with a participle and a form of "to be" (Smyth §408), so the game leaves that cell out.
