# The article

The definite article has a masculine, a feminine and a neuter, and every case except the vocative (Smyth §332). Its masculine and neuter go like the 2nd declension, and its feminine like the 1st declension nouns in η. That makes it a good first paradigm: once you know it, you already know most of the endings of {{word noun:λόγος}}, {{word noun:δῶρον}} and {{word noun:τιμή}}.

## What to notice
- The masculine and feminine nominatives, singular and plural, start with a rough breathing instead of τ, and carry no accent: {{form article:ὁ nom.sg.m nom.sg.f nom.pl.m nom.pl.f}}. Every other form starts with τ.
- The neuter has a single form for the nominative and the accusative in each number, as every neuter word does (Smyth §202).
- The genitive and dative have a circumflex, like the other 1st and 2nd declension words accented on their last syllable (Smyth §207): {{form article:ὁ gen.sg.m dat.sg.f}}.
- In the dual one form serves all three genders: {{form article:ὁ nom.du.m}} for the nominative and accusative, {{form article:ὁ gen.du.m}} for the genitive and dative.
