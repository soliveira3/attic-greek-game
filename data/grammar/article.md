# The article

Greek has one article, which agrees with its noun in gender, number and case. It has no vocative (Smyth §332). In shape it is a short version of the nouns you meet first: the masculine and neuter look like the 2nd declension, the feminine like the 1st declension nouns in η. Learn it first and most of the endings of {{word noun:λόγος}}, {{word noun:δῶρον}} and {{word noun:τιμή}} come for free.

## What to notice
- Four forms have a rough breathing where the others have τ, and no accent at all: the masculine and feminine nominatives, {{form article:ὁ nom.sg.m nom.sg.f nom.pl.m nom.pl.f}}. Smyth calls such words proclitics, because they lean on the word that follows (Smyth §179).
- The neuter uses one form for the nominative and the accusative in each number, like every neuter word (Smyth §202).
- The genitive and dative carry a circumflex, as other 1st and 2nd declension words accented on the last syllable do (Smyth §207): {{form article:ὁ gen.sg.m dat.sg.f}}.
- The dual has a single form for all three genders in each pair of cases: {{form article:ὁ nom.du.m}} (nominative and accusative), {{form article:ὁ gen.du.m}} (genitive and dative). The separate feminine dual forms are very rare in Attic prose (Smyth §332).
