# Middle and passive

Greek has three voices: active, middle and passive (Smyth §356). In the middle the subject acts on itself or for itself; in the passive the subject is acted on (Smyth §1735).

## Shared forms
The passive borrows all its forms from the middle except in the future and the aorist (Smyth §356). So in the present, the imperfect and the perfect one set of forms is both middle and passive; this game calls it middle/passive.

In Parse it, middle or passive is accepted for these forms. In Make it and Fill the table they are asked for as middle/passive.

## Future and aorist
These tenses have separate middle and passive forms.
- The aorist passive adds -θη- to the verb stem and takes active endings (Smyth §585): {{form verb:λύω aor.pass.ind.1.sg}}. Compare the aorist middle {{form verb:λύω aor.mid.ind.1.sg}}.
- The future passive adds σ and the thematic vowel to the aorist passive stem, with middle endings (Smyth §589): {{form verb:λύω fut.pass.ind.1.sg}}. Compare the future middle {{form verb:λύω fut.mid.ind.1.sg}}.
- Some verbs form a 2nd aorist passive, with -η- and no θ (Smyth §590, §594): {{form verb:γράφω aor.pass.ind.1.sg}}.

## Deponents
Deponent verbs have middle (or passive) forms but an active meaning (Smyth §356): {{word verb:βούλομαι}}, {{word verb:γίγνομαι}}. They have no active forms to drill.
