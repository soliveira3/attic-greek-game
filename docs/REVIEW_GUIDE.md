# Review guide

Automated checks show that the game's forms match Wiktionary, that Morpheus agrees with them,
and that the model paradigms match Smyth. They cannot show that the drills teach well, or catch
every doubtful form. Two short human reviews can. This page describes both.

Report what you find through the
[wrong-form issue form](https://github.com/soliveira3/attic-greek-game/issues/new?template=wrong-form.yml).
Notes in any format sent to the maintainer work too.

---

## 1. For a Greek teacher (about an hour)

Nothing to install: open the live site in any browser.

### a. Forms (30 minutes)
[REVIEW_SAMPLE.md](REVIEW_SAMPLE.md) lists 40 forms drawn at random from the game's data. It
covers nouns, adjectives and verbs in proportion to the word list. The model paradigms, already
compared with Smyth, are left out. For each row, mark the last column:

- ✓ the form is right for Attic prose;
- ✗ it is wrong (please give the form you expect and your source);
- ? it is possible but not what you would teach (rare, poetic, later, or a less usual
  alternative).

Vowel-length marks are shown as the game shows them by default. A missing length mark is not an
error.

### b. Answer checking (10 minutes)
Play a few rounds of **Make it** and give some answers wrong on purpose:
- a wrong accent,
- a missing augment,
- a wrong ending,
- the right form of a different case or person.

Does the feedback name the mistake fairly? The rules are in [SPEC.md §6](SPEC.md). The default
mode points out accents but does not count them.

### c. Grammar notes (15 minutes)
Read two or three notes (**Notes** in the menu). Suggested: *The 2nd declension*, *The augment*,
*Principal parts*. Check:
- Is anything wrong or misleading for a first-year student?
- Do the Smyth section numbers point to the right place?

### d. Order of the path (5 minutes)
**Learn** lists 17 units in the order a first-year course usually meets them. Would you teach
them in a different order?

## 2. For learners (20 minutes each, two or three people)

Use people who have had at least a few weeks of Greek. Sit with them, or ask them to note their
thoughts as they go. Do not explain the game first.

1. Open the site and do whatever seems natural for two minutes. *Was it clear where to start?*
2. Take **Find my level**. *Did the result feel right?*
3. Play one lesson to the end. *Was typing Greek easy? Did the feedback help?*
4. Get one answer wrong on purpose and follow the link to the grammar note. *Was the note
   useful at that moment?*
5. Play one round of **Fill the table** and one **Endings sprint**. *Which mode would you come
   back to, and why?*
6. On a phone, play a lesson. *Anything hard to tap or read?*

Then ask:
- What was the most frustrating moment?
- What would make you open this again tomorrow?
- Did any form or explanation look wrong?

Write down what they did, not only what they said. A long pause or a wrong tap tells you more
than an opinion.
