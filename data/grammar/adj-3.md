# Adjectives of the 3rd declension

An adjective declined only in the 3rd declension has one set of forms for the masculine and feminine together and another for the neuter (Smyth §291). Nearly all of them belong to one of two types. In the first the stem ends in ες, and the nominatives end in -ης and -ες; in the second it ends in ον, with nominatives in -ων and -ον.

## Stems in ες
{{word adj:ἀληθής}} follows the σ-stem nouns: its σ was lost between vowels and the vowels merged (Smyth §292, §263). See [σ-stems](decl3-s).

## Stems in ον
{{word adj:εὐδαίμων}} follows the nasal stems, with the endings -ος, -ι, -α in the singular and -ες, -ων, -σι, -ας in the plural (Smyth §293). Its closest noun model is {{word noun:δαίμων}}, which, like the adjective, has a vocative without the long vowel: {{form adj:εὐδαίμων voc.sg.m}}. See [consonant stems](decl3-consonant).

{{table adj:εὐδαίμων}}

## What to notice
- The neuter nominative, accusative and vocative singular is the bare stem: {{form adj:ἀληθής nom.sg.n}}, {{form adj:εὐδαίμων nom.sg.n}}. In the ον-stems the neuter also moves its accent as far back as it can (Smyth §293): {{form adj:εὐδαίμων nom.sg.m nom.sg.n}}.
- In the neuter plural those cases end in -α, or in -η where ε and α have merged: {{form adj:εὐδαίμων nom.pl.n}}, {{form adj:ἀληθής nom.pl.n}}.
