# The 2nd declension

Nouns of the 2nd declension have stems ending in ο (Smyth §228). In the nominative singular the masculines take -ς and the neuters -ν. A handful of feminines, among them {{word noun:ὁδός}}, follow the masculine pattern exactly.

## What to notice
- The genitive singular ends in -ου, and the dative singular has an iota subscript: {{form noun:λόγος gen.sg dat.sg}} (Smyth §229).
- Masculines and feminines have a vocative singular of their own, in -ε: {{form noun:λόγος voc.sg}}, {{form noun:ὁδός voc.sg}}. Two exceptions are common: the word for "god" uses its nominative as vocative, and the word for "brother" moves its accent back (Smyth §233): {{form noun:θεός voc.sg}}, {{form noun:ἀδελφός voc.sg}}. In the plural the vocative is always the nominative (Smyth §202).
- A neuter uses one form for the nominative, accusative and vocative in each number; in the plural it ends in -α: {{form noun:δῶρον nom.pl}} (Smyth §202, §228).
- The accent stays where the nominative has it unless a long last syllable forces it one syllable toward the end (Smyth §205). Nouns accented on the last syllable take a circumflex in the genitive and dative (Smyth §207): {{form noun:ὁδός nom.sg gen.sg}}. See [accents and breathings](accent).
