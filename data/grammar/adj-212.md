# Adjectives of the 1st and 2nd declensions

The commonest adjectives borrow their endings from nouns you already know. In the masculine and the neuter they go like the 2nd declension, {{word noun:λόγος}} and {{word noun:δῶρον}}; in the feminine like the 1st, {{word noun:τιμή}} or {{word noun:χώρα}} (Smyth §286). The rule for the feminine vowel is the nouns' rule: α after ε, ι or ρ, η otherwise (Smyth §286, §218). Compare {{word adj:ἀγαθός}} with {{word adj:ἄξιος}}.

## What to notice
- In the feminine nominative and genitive plural an adjective keeps the masculine's accent, which a noun would not do (Smyth §287, §209). The noun {{word noun:αἰτία}} has {{form noun:αἰτία gen.pl}}, but the adjective {{word adj:αἴτιος}} has {{form adj:αἴτιος gen.pl.f}}, and {{form adj:ἄξιος nom.pl.f}} in the nominative plural.
- Otherwise the accent stays on the syllable it has in the masculine nominative singular, unless a long last syllable moves it (Smyth §205): {{form adj:ἄξιος nom.sg.m nom.sg.f gen.sg.m}}.
- A few adjectives of this kind may also use the masculine forms for the feminine, as two-ending adjectives do (Smyth §289): {{form adj:ἴδιος nom.sg.f}}. See [two-ending adjectives](adj-22).
