# Adjectives of the 1st and 2nd declensions

Most adjectives have three endings, -ος, -η (or -α), -ον. The masculine and neuter are declined like the 2nd declension and the feminine like the 1st (Smyth §286). After ε, ι or ρ the feminine has α instead of η, just as in the nouns (Smyth §286, §218): compare {{word adj:ἀγαθός}} with {{word adj:ἄξιος}}.

## What to notice
- Each gender follows its noun model: {{word noun:λόγος}} for the masculine, {{word noun:τιμή}} or {{word noun:χώρα}} for the feminine, {{word noun:δῶρον}} for the neuter. See [the 1st declension](decl1) and [the 2nd declension](decl2).
- Unlike the nouns of the 1st declension, the feminine genitive plural has the same accent and form as the masculine and neuter (Smyth §209, §208): {{form adj:ἄξιος gen.pl.m gen.pl.f}}.
- The accent stays on the syllable it has in the masculine nominative singular, unless a long last syllable moves it (Smyth §205): {{form adj:ἄξιος nom.sg.m nom.sg.f gen.sg.m}}.
