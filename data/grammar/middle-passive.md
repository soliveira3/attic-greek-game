# Middle and passive

Greek verbs have three voices: active, middle and passive (Smyth §356). In the middle the subject does something to or for itself; in the passive something is done to the subject (Smyth §1735).

## Shared forms
Only the future and the aorist have passive forms of their own. In every other tense the passive simply uses the middle's forms (Smyth §356), so the present, imperfect and perfect each have one set of forms that is both middle and passive. This game calls it middle/passive.

In Parse it, either middle or passive is accepted for these forms; in Make it and Fill the table they are asked for as middle/passive.

## Future and aorist
These two tenses keep middle and passive apart.
- The aorist passive adds -θη- to the verb stem, with endings like the active's (Smyth §585): {{form verb:λύω aor.pass.ind.1.sg}}. The aorist middle is a different form: {{form verb:λύω aor.mid.ind.1.sg}}.
- The future passive is built on the aorist passive stem, adding σ, the thematic vowel and middle endings (Smyth §589): {{form verb:λύω fut.pass.ind.1.sg}}. Compare the future middle: {{form verb:λύω fut.mid.ind.1.sg}}.
- A few verbs have a 2nd aorist passive, with -η- but no θ (Smyth §590, §594): {{form verb:γράφω aor.pass.ind.1.sg}}.

## Deponents
Some verbs are translated as actives although most of their forms look middle or passive; they are called deponents (Smyth §356): {{word verb:βούλομαι}}, {{word verb:γίγνομαι}}. Some of them still have an active aorist or perfect: {{form verb:γίγνομαι perf.act.ind.1.sg}}, {{form verb:ἔρχομαι aor.act.ind.1.sg}}. Others have an aorist that looks passive but means something active; Parse it calls such a form passive, after its shape: {{form verb:βούλομαι aor.pass.ind.1.sg}}.

The principal parts show which voices a verb has: see [tense stems and principal parts](principal-parts).
