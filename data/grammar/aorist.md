# The 1st and 2nd aorist

## The 1st aorist
Most verbs make their aorist by putting -σα after the verb stem (Smyth §542):

{{table verb:λύω aor.act}}

{{table verb:λύω aor.mid}}

The σ meets the last sound of the stem and combines with it (Smyth §544–545):
- labial and σ give ψ, velar and σ give ξ: {{form verb:πέμπω aor.act.ind.1.sg}}, {{form verb:πράττω aor.act.ind.1.sg}};
- a dental vanishes before σ: {{form verb:πείθω aor.act.ind.1.sg}};
- after λ, μ, ν or ρ the σ itself is lost, and the vowel of the stem grows long to make up for it: {{form verb:μένω aor.act.ind.1.sg}}, {{form verb:φαίνω aor.act.ind.1.sg}}.

## The 2nd aorist
The 2nd aorist has no -σα. The thematic vowel is added directly to the verb stem, which is often shorter than the stem of the present (Smyth §546–547). Since the indicative takes the same endings as the imperfect, only the stem tells the two tenses apart:

{{table verb:λείπω aor.act}}

- Imperfect {{form verb:λείπω impf.act.ind.1.sg}}, aorist {{form verb:λείπω aor.act.ind.1.sg}}; likewise {{form verb:φεύγω impf.act.ind.1.sg aor.act.ind.1.sg}}.
- The middle works the same way: {{form verb:λείπω impf.mp.ind.1.sg aor.mid.ind.1.sg}}.
- The 2nd aorist active infinitive is accented on its ending, the middle infinitive on the syllable before it (Smyth §425, §469): {{form verb:λείπω aor.act.inf aor.mid.inf}}. See [infinitives](infinitives).

Most verbs have only one kind of aorist. {{word verb:φέρω}} has both, made on another root: a form used as a 1st aorist, though it has κ where the σ would be (Smyth §544), and a 2nd aorist: {{form verb:φέρω aor.act.ind.1.sg}}.

Like every past tense, the aorist takes the [augment](augment) in the indicative.
