# The 1st declension

All 1st declension nouns have stems ending in a long α (Smyth §211). The feminines end in -α or -η in the nominative singular; the masculines add -ς, giving -ας or -ης.

## Feminines
What happens to the vowel in the singular depends on the sound before it (Smyth §217–219, §224):
- After ε, ι or ρ, the singular keeps α in every case, as in {{word noun:χώρα}}.
- After any other sound, the singular has η in every case, as in {{word noun:τιμή}}.
- Some nouns have a short α in the nominative, accusative and vocative singular. After σ, ξ, ψ, ττ, ζ or λλ the genitive and dative singular then switch to η, as in {{word noun:θάλαττα}}.
- Others with a short α in those three cases keep α in the genitive and dative, as many nouns ending in α after ει do: {{form noun:ἀλήθεια nom.sg gen.sg dat.sg}} (Smyth §219).

Whatever happens in the singular, the plural is the same for all of them: compare {{form noun:χώρα nom.pl}}, {{form noun:τιμή nom.pl}} and {{form noun:θάλαττα nom.pl}}. The genitive plural always has a circumflex on its last syllable, wherever the nominative is accented (Smyth §208): {{form noun:θάλαττα gen.pl}}. See [accents and breathings](accent).

## Masculines
A masculine adds ς to the nominative singular and borrows the genitive singular in -ου from the 2nd declension (Smyth §222, §225). After ε, ι or ρ it has α in the singular, as in {{word noun:νεανίας}}; otherwise η, as in {{word noun:πολίτης}} (Smyth §224). The vocative of a masculine in -ης with τ before it ends in a short α (Smyth §226): {{form noun:πολίτης voc.sg}}. One common noun also moves its accent back in the vocative (Smyth §223): {{form noun:δεσπότης nom.sg voc.sg}}.
