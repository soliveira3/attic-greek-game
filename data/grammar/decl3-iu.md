# 3rd declension: ι- and υ-stems

Most ι-stems, and some υ-stems, show their stem vowel only in the nominative, accusative and vocative singular. In the other cases they have ε instead, and the genitive singular ends in -ως rather than -ος (Smyth §268–270). {{word noun:πόλις}} is the model for the ι-stems; {{word noun:ἄστυ}} is a neuter υ-stem.

## What to notice
- The accusative singular adds ν to the stem instead of -α (Smyth §246): {{form noun:πόλις acc.sg}}.
- The vocative singular is the bare stem (Smyth §248): {{form noun:πόλις voc.sg}}.
- The genitive in -ως keeps the accent three syllables from the end, although its last syllable is long (Smyth §163, §271): {{form noun:πόλις gen.sg gen.pl}}.
- The accusative plural borrows the nominative plural (Smyth §272): {{form noun:πόλις nom.pl acc.pl}}.
- The neuter has one form for the nominative, accusative and vocative in each number: {{form noun:ἄστυ nom.sg nom.pl}}.
