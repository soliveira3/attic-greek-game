# Accents and breathings

## Breathings
A word that begins with a vowel or a diphthong always has a breathing over it: the rough breathing, pronounced as h, or the smooth breathing, which is silent (Smyth §9). An initial υ always has the rough breathing (Smyth §10), and so does an initial ρ (Smyth §13). On a diphthong the breathing goes over its second vowel, {{form verb:αἰσθάνομαι pres.mp.ind.1.sg}}, but a long vowel with an iota subscript carries it itself (Smyth §11): {{form verb:αἰσθάνομαι impf.mp.ind.1.sg}}.

## The three accents
- The acute may stand on any of the last three syllables (Smyth §149).
- The circumflex needs a long vowel or a diphthong, and stands only on one of the last two syllables (Smyth §149).
- The grave takes the place of an acute on the last syllable when another word follows (Smyth §154). The game always shows words on their own, so you will see, and type, the acute there.

If the last syllable is long, the accent cannot go further back than the next-to-last (Smyth §163). An accented long next-to-last syllable has the circumflex when the last syllable is short, and the acute when it is long (Smyth §164). For this purpose a final -αι or -οι counts as short (Smyth §169).

## Nouns and adjectives: persistent accent
In nouns and adjectives the accent tries to stay on the syllable it has in the nominative singular, moving only when the last syllable requires it (Smyth §205): {{form noun:θάλαττα nom.sg gen.sg nom.pl}}. 1st and 2nd declension words accented on the last syllable change to a circumflex in the genitive and dative (Smyth §207): {{form noun:τιμή nom.sg gen.sg dat.sg}}. The genitive plural of the 1st declension always has its circumflex on the last syllable (Smyth §208): {{form noun:χώρα gen.pl}}.

## Verbs: recessive accent
A finite verb puts its accent as far from the end as the last syllable lets it (Smyth §423): {{form verb:πείθω pres.act.ind.1.sg pres.act.ind.1.pl}}, {{form verb:λείπω impf.mp.ind.1.sg}}. Two things limit this:
- in a compound, the accent cannot go back past the augment (Smyth §426): {{form verb:παρέχω impf.act.ind.1.sg}}, {{form verb:ὑπάρχω impf.act.ind.1.sg}};
- a contracted future keeps the accent of the vowels that merged (Smyth §424): {{form verb:μένω fut.act.ind.1.sg}}.

Infinitives follow their own rules: see [infinitives](infinitives).

## In the game
With the default setting, ignore accents, a wrong accent is pointed out but the answer still counts, unless the accent turns it into another form of the same word, as with the present typed for a contracted future (see [thematic verb endings](verb-endings)); that is marked wrong. Breathings and the iota subscript count in every mode except lenient. Iota written on the line, ωι for ῳ, is accepted with a note, except in strict mode, which also counts accents.
