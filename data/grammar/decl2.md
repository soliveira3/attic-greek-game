# The 2nd declension

The 2nd declension has stems in ο (Smyth §228). Masculines add ς in the nominative singular and neuters add ν. The few feminines, such as {{word noun:ὁδός}}, are declined exactly like the masculines.

## What to notice
- The genitive singular ends in -ου and the dative singular has an iota subscript: {{form noun:λόγος gen.sg dat.sg}} (Smyth §229).
- The masculine has its own vocative singular, in -ε: {{form noun:λόγος voc.sg}}. In the plural the vocative is always the same as the nominative (Smyth §202).
- A neuter has one form for the nominative, accusative and vocative in each number, and in the plural that form ends in -α: {{form noun:δῶρον nom.pl}} (Smyth §202, §228).
- The accent stays on the syllable it has in the nominative, unless a long last syllable pushes it forward (Smyth §205). Words accented on the last syllable take a circumflex in the genitive and dative (Smyth §207): {{form noun:ὁδός nom.sg gen.sg}}. See [accents and breathings](accent).
