# 3rd declension: ι- and υ-stems

{{word noun:πόλις}} keeps the ι of its stem only in the three cases whose ending is -ς, -ν or nothing at all: the nominative, accusative and vocative singular. In all the other cases you find an ε, and the genitive singular ends in -ως instead of the usual 3rd declension -ος (Smyth §268–270). Some υ-stems work the same way; {{word noun:ἄστυ}} is a neuter example.

## What to notice
- The accusative singular ends in ν, not -α (Smyth §246): {{form noun:πόλις acc.sg}}.
- The vocative singular is the bare stem (Smyth §248): {{form noun:πόλις voc.sg}}.
- The genitives singular and plural are accented three syllables from the end, although the last syllable is long: this is allowed in these nouns (Smyth §163, §271): {{form noun:πόλις gen.sg gen.pl}}.
- The nominative and accusative plural are spelled alike, because the accusative takes over the nominative's form (Smyth §272): {{form noun:πόλις nom.pl acc.pl}}.
- In the neuter, nominative, accusative and vocative share one form in each number: {{form noun:ἄστυ nom.sg}}, {{form noun:ἄστυ nom.pl}}.
