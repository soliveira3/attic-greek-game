# The 1st declension

The 1st declension has stems in long α (Smyth §211). Its feminines end in -α or -η in the nominative singular; its masculines add -ς and end in -ας or -ης.

## Feminines
Which vowel a feminine shows in the singular depends on the letter before it (Smyth §217–219):
- After ε, ι or ρ, a long α runs through the whole singular, as in {{word noun:χώρα}}.
- Otherwise η runs through the singular, as in {{word noun:τιμή}}.
- Some nouns have a short α in the nominative, accusative and vocative singular and switch to η in the genitive and dative singular. This happens after a double consonant such as ττ, ξ or ψ, and after ζ or λλ, as in {{word noun:θάλαττα}}.

The plural is the same for all three: compare {{form noun:χώρα nom.pl}}, {{form noun:τιμή nom.pl}} and {{form noun:θάλαττα nom.pl}}. The genitive plural of every 1st declension noun has a circumflex on its last syllable, wherever the accent was in the nominative (Smyth §208): {{form noun:θάλαττα gen.pl}}.

## Masculines
Masculines add ς in the nominative singular and take their genitive singular in -ου from the 2nd declension (Smyth §222, §225). Those in -ας keep α in the singular, as {{word noun:νεανίας}}; those in -ης have η, as {{word noun:πολίτης}}. Those whose nominative ends in -ης after τ have a vocative in short α: {{form noun:πολίτης voc.sg}} (Smyth §226).
