# Two-ending adjectives

Some adjectives of the 2nd declension never developed a feminine of their own: a feminine noun simply takes the masculine forms. They therefore have two sets of endings instead of three (Smyth §288). Most are compounds, like {{word adj:ἄδικος}}, built from the negative prefix ἀ- and the root of {{word noun:δίκη}} (Smyth §289).

{{table adj:ἄδικος}}

## What to notice
- Masculine and feminine are always identical: {{form adj:ἄδικος nom.sg.m nom.sg.f}}.
- The neuter goes like {{word noun:δῶρον}}: {{form adj:ἄδικος nom.sg.n nom.pl.n}}.
- The masculine and feminine vocative singular ends in -ε, as in the [2nd declension](decl2): {{form adj:ἄδικος voc.sg.m}}.
